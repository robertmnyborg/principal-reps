# The pattern, and the card

The thing to memorize. Everything else in this skill exists to make these five beats
automatic under time pressure.

## The sentence

**What wasn't defined, what I claim, what I'd measure, whether it can be undone, what
would prove me wrong.**

Say it in your head before you open your mouth. Five beats, in order. The middle of any
answer is your existing domain competence. These five are the frame that makes it read at
Principal altitude instead of Senior.

## The beats

**1. Undefined — the first fifteen seconds.**
> "The thing nobody had decided here is ___."

This is the single highest-leverage habit. At Principal level the business opportunity
and the product strategy are often not yet defined, and the problem itself may be unclear
before you start. Opening on what was undefined shows you can work there. Opening on what
you built reads one level lower.

**2. Claim — a sentence someone can disagree with.**
> "My claim is ___."

Test: could a competent person in the room say "no, I think the opposite"? If not, it is
a description, not a claim. "Substitution matters" fails. "Substitution is a trust lever,
not a basket lever" passes.

**3. Metric — one, and say per what.**
> "I'd measure ___, per ___."

One. Naming three names none. Then say what it does when the mechanism works perfectly
and the headline number still doesn't move, because that is the question behind the
question.

Immediately stress-test your own metric out loud: **can this improve by doing less?** If
yes, name the guard metric in the same breath. Precision needs a recall term.

**4. Reversibility — can it be undone, and at what price.**
> "This is cheap to undo, so I'd just run it. / This is hard to undo because ___, so I'd
> spend ___ to be sure first."

The most commonly skipped beat, and the cheapest to fix. Say out loud whether the decision
is reversible at least once in any substantive answer. Contracts, data models, taxonomies,
and public commitments are the usual irreversible decisions. Almost everything else can be
undone and should be shipped rather than debated.

**5. Test — the cheapest thing that could prove you wrong.**
> "The cheapest way to check this is ___, and I'd be wrong if ___."

The test and the falsifier are one beat, because a test that cannot fail is not a test.
Default to an experiment over an analysis: a randomized holdout beats a year of
observational pre-post, costs less, and survives challenge.

## The anti-patterns

Observed failure modes, scored across real reps. Each one reads as Senior rather than
Principal, and each has a mechanical fix.

| Tell | Fix |
|---|---|
| An absolute end-state claim ("X becomes the only Y", "every Z should have W") | State the boundary in the same sentence, or don't state the absolute |
| Climbing back to strategy when the metric gets hard | Notice the drift, say "let me stay at the mechanism," and answer |
| "We'd train a model to..." | Name the label, the ground-truth source, and what threshold the consequence demands |
| Every rung owned by the same team | Ownership of the outcome is not ownership of the dependency. Name the partner org and the ask |
| Naming a failure without its cost | Failures are priced, not listed |
| A number with no evidence label | Say verified, reported, assumed, or unverified, out loud |
| A metric named without its error direction | Say which of the two errors you refuse to make and what each one costs. A gate with one direction priced drifts toward the quieter error, and the quieter error is the one that ships |
| "Better" with no rubric | Name the dimensions and who agreed to them. A quality bar one person wrote is an opinion wearing a metric's clothes |
| An accuracy number with no ground truth behind it | Say who labeled it, against what guideline, and on which cuts. A score on a balanced set decides nothing when the real distribution is 99% easy |

## Where the answer must land

For anything with a physical operation behind it, the answer is not finished until it
lands in the building, the field, the truck, or the dock. Software answers to physical
constraints are the most common way a strong candidate reads as an armchair PM.

## The endgame

This tool aims at its own obsolescence. You are done when you can run all five beats from
a printed card and a kitchen timer, with no model in the loop. Track it, and stop when
you get there.
